Why doesn't ChatGPT mention my business?

Your business can be established, legitimate and good at what it does—and still be absent from an AI answer. One missing mention does not tell you why.

By PRIZIX · October 27, 2026

The short answer

Start by checking the question, whether web search was used, the accuracy of your public information and whether your site can be accessed. Being missing from an answer is an observation—not proof that your website is blocked, penalized or technically broken.

It is tempting to ask ChatGPT for a recommendation, see a competitor named and conclude that your business needs “AI optimization.” But the useful next step is not immediately rewriting every page. It is understanding what you tested and what you can actually improve.

This guide focuses on business discovery in AI-assisted search. It does not describe a way to control a model's answers or guarantee that a particular company gets recommended.

What to check1. What question did you ask?2. Was web search involved?3. Is your business information clear?4. Can search crawlers access your site?5. What did the answer actually show?6. What should you improve first?

1. Start with the question—not the missing name.

“Recommend a web designer” and “Who can redesign an established Shopify store with a complicated product catalog?” are different requests. They describe different needs. A general answer is not an exhaustive directory of every qualified provider.

Choose questions that reflect how a prospective customer might describe the problem, the location if relevant, and the type of help needed. Avoid making the question artificially narrow just to produce your company's name.

Illustrative example—not a visibility test result

A dental practice asking for help after an ownership change needs something more specific than generic marketing advice. A useful test question would describe outdated practice information and the transition problem, rather than simply asking for “the best agency.”

Also separate branded questions from unbranded ones. Asking “What is PRIZIX?” checks brand representation. Asking for companies that provide a service checks a different discovery scenario. Success on the first does not establish visibility on the second.

2. Check whether the answer involved web search.

A response generated without a current web search should not be treated as a live review of your website. Record which feature or mode you used and whether search activity or source links were shown.

Even when sources are present, do not assume that every business in a category was reviewed. Open the cited pages and check what they actually support. A competitor's mention can show you what information appeared in that answer; it does not reveal a complete ranking formula.

Be precise about the question you are investigating: “Does this answer describe us accurately?” is different from “Does it cite our website?” and different again from “Does it recommend us to a potential customer?”

3. Make the business easy for people to understand.

Before looking for a special AI technique, review the information a visitor would find on your website and important public profiles:

  • Identity: Is the business name consistent, with a clear explanation of who operates it?
  • Services: Do separate pages explain what you offer and the situations you help with?
  • Audience: Is it clear which customers the service is suitable for?
  • Evidence: Are project descriptions and experience accurate and specific?
  • Contact: Is there a usable next step for someone considering your service?
  • Consistency: Do old descriptions or profiles contradict the current offer?

These checks are worthwhile because they improve the underlying information. They are not proof that changing a sentence will cause ChatGPT to recommend you.

If your homepage is the only place that briefly lists five different services, more useful service pages may help people evaluate the business. Repeating the same keyword across dozens of near-identical pages is not a substitute for that clarity.

4. Check access—but do not confuse access with selection.

For ChatGPT search, OpenAI's publisher guidance says not to block OAI-SearchBot if you want content included in summaries and snippets. A technical review should consider both robots.txt and hosting or security rules that might prevent access.

Allowing a crawler does not guarantee that your page will be selected or cited for a particular question. Equally, you cannot diagnose a crawler block just because the business is absent from one answer. Ask for technical evidence before treating it as an access problem.

Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode are separate features. Google's official guidance says ordinary SEO practices remain relevant and special AI markup is not required. Do not assume a requirement or result from one platform applies to all the others.

If technical changes are needed, document what is being changed and why. Search access, content indexing and model training are not interchangeable concepts.

5. Record what the answer actually shows.

A useful baseline distinguishes three observations:

Different observations answer different business questions
ObservationWhat it tells youWhat it does not establish
Brand mentionThe answer names your company.That your site was cited or your service recommended.
Website citationA source link points to your page.That the answer endorses your business.
RecommendationThe answer presents your business as an option for that question.That other users or questions will receive the same answer.

Keep the exact prompt, date, platform, relevant settings and answer. Where available, record cited URLs and whether the description is factually correct. A screenshot without the question or date is much less useful.

Repeat a defined set of checks when reviewing progress. Do not keep changing the wording until the business appears and then call that an improvement. Responses can change for reasons unrelated to your edits, so a small sample should not be presented as comprehensive market visibility.

6. Choose improvements you can justify.

Start with concrete gaps: inaccurate business details, missing service explanations, inaccessible pages or useful questions that your content never answers. Prioritize corrections you can verify instead of buying a vague promise of “AI authority.”

A sensible first phase might include a documented baseline, a review of core pages and profiles, technical checks where needed, and a short list of agreed changes. Then repeat the checks and report what was observed—not what you hoped would happen.

Be cautious about guarantees of first place, a fixed number of recommendations or a result by a specific date. You can control your published information and the work completed; you cannot control every AI platform's answers.
