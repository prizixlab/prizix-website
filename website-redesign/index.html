Refresh or rebuild?

Not every outdated website needs a new platform.

A refresh may be enough when the structure is sound but the copy, imagery or selected layouts need attention. A larger redesign may make sense when the site is difficult to maintain, the page structure no longer fits the business, or important functionality is missing.

Moving platforms is a separate decision. We consider maintenance, integrations and the work involved in preserving useful content before recommending it.

A simple scope comparison

Focused refresh: Improve a defined set of pages or sections while keeping the main structure.

Full redesign: Rework the structure, content presentation and page templates across the site.

Platform move: Rebuild on a different foundation when there is a clear operational reason.

Questions before a redesign

What to know before starting.

Can you keep my current domain and branding?

Usually, yes. A redesign does not require a new business name, logo or domain. We review what should stay and identify any access or technical constraints.

Will my website be offline while you redesign it?

Where the platform supports it, work can be prepared separately from the public site. Launch arrangements and any expected interruption are agreed for your project; zero downtime is not an automatic promise.

Will a redesign improve my Google rankings?

It may address content and technical weaknesses, but a new design alone does not guarantee rankings. We scope relevant search-related work and plan for changed URLs where needed.

Can you rewrite the website copy?

Yes, copy review or writing can be included. You provide accurate business details and approve the final descriptions of your services, experience and claims.

Can we start with just the homepage or a few pages?

Yes. A focused first phase can be appropriate when the main problems are concentrated in a small part of the site. We make clear what remains outside that phase.

How long does a website redesign take?

We give a project-specific schedule based on scope, content readiness, platform requirements and approval timing. We do not set a deadline before understanding those factors.
